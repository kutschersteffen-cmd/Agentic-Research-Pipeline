# Writing guide

- Headlines are conclusions: a claim with a verb and a number, not a topic label.
- Use plain verbs ("use", "cut", "rose"). Write numbers as digits. Name the specific company, sector or figure.
- Hedge only on real uncertainty, once, where it applies. No stock words (leverage, robust, landscape, crucial), no "not X but Y", no exclamation marks, at most one dash per slide.
- Do not open several slots with the same word. Do not label every bullet "Word:". Vary list lengths.
- Use only numbers that appear in the notes or datasets.

## By audience level
- executive: lead with the decision and the headline number; leave out method.
- technical: give the method, the caveat and the exact figure.
- general: short words, no jargon; explain any term of art once.

## By tone
- formal: full sentences, no contractions.
- conversational: contractions and direct address are fine.
- persuasive: lead with the strongest sourced fact; no superlatives.
- neutral_analytical: state findings and their size; no adjectives of praise or alarm.

## Before / after
- Before: "The portfolio's emissions landscape is evolving, with robust progress across holdings."
  After: "Portfolio emissions fell 12% in 2024; utilities drove most of it."
- Before: "Decision Studio unlocks a seamless, holistic tiering experience."
  After: "Decision Studio sorts each holding into one of three tiers, by evidence strength."
- Before: "This theme universe is not just broad, but pivotal to the transition."
  After: "The theme universe covers 240 companies, 60 of them in grid equipment."
